# Milestone 3.7 — intro and chapter-transition follow-up

## Findings and fixes

The eight permanent renderer-side PointLight slots remain unchanged. An audit of lights outside that budget found three **conditional direct PointLights**: the facility specimen tube becomes visible at M3, the intro cabin becomes visible at alternating camera shots, and the ship is removed on M4 departure. The specimen light changed Three.js's point-light shader define at M2→M3 (`2,35,4` → `2,36,4` in the Firefox program keys), provoking eight or more otherwise identical material variants. Those lights are now authored *sources* for the existing eight slots. Light selection also rejects detached objects so a removed ship/cabin cannot stay eligible. Only intensity, color and slot positions vary during play.

The two remaining M3 first-use variants in the headless Firefox reproduction were **MeshBasicMaterial impacts and LineBasicMaterial sparks** generated by the new interior encounter. The exact reusable pool materials are precompiled against the established campaign light/fog configuration when gameplay first begins; no visible dummy geometry or movement-time compilation is added. The intro cabin materials are precompiled at the M1 launch while the menu still covers the scene. Diagnostic-only `shaderPrograms` events now include bounded program keys and mesh/material owners when available.

The new diagnostic events `introCut` (shot, cabin/exterior, camera and current program count), `mainThreadHeartbeatLag`, `introCabinPrewarm`, and `combatPrewarm` separate view changes and JavaScript timing from renderer calls. `startIntro`, `updateIntro`, `introShotCam`, `endIntro`, `beginGameplay`, `drawIntro`, SFX initialization and render/postrender are individually timed. Optional `?perfDiagnostics=1&perfGpuSync=1` adds `gl.finish()` **after each render** to attribute deferred GPU/driver work; it is deliberately off during normal play and should be used only for a controlled second diagnostic run.

M2 Mission Select also left the menu backdrop render-visible after its DOM overlay closed, adding four submitted point lights (38 rather than the campaign's stable 34). Gameplay now hides the backdrop. Three already-authored materials that otherwise first linked along the A–E route (Beta's dish, facility dust, containment floor ring) are compiled once against the settled light/fog state at gameplay handoff. The geometry and materials remain persistent. This is an initial-load tradeoff, not movement-time allocation or light-count switching.

## Firefox headless reproduction (software WebGL)

With `tests/transition-diagnose.mjs firefox`, the pre-fix M2→M3 reproduction compiled ten new programs (`35` → `36` submitted point lights); another run with the specimen light budgeted had two first-use basic effect programs and a ~130–160 ms render interval. After the effect prewarm, **M2→M3: zero links, zero new programs, worst observed 83 ms** in the final GPU-sync diagnostic run. M3→M4 and the actual M4 purge → board → departure/result also linked **zero** programs in that run. The precise headless frame timings vary considerably with software rendering; these numbers are not desktop FPS estimates.

## Supplied desktop Firefox 156 intro trace

The provided `chargefront-perf-1790227502540_20260924_Intro.json` separates **pre-launch menu throttling** from **actual cutscene freezes**. Before the New Campaign click, the scene had only 13 roots, no solids/relays, five menu shader programs and no `introCut` events. Periodic RAF gaps of ~1–9 seconds had 11–16 ms render calls and 0–1 ms updates. The 100 ms heartbeat timer was also delayed by ~900 ms repeatedly *while RAF ran normally at other times*: a timer/RAF scheduling or tab-focus throttle, not evidence of a continuously blocked JavaScript main thread or a spacecraft camera cut. The trace alone does not prove why Firefox deprioritized that menu window.

At campaign launch the actual world build took 856 ms, cabin compile setup 18 ms, `startIntro` 26 ms and `playIntroSfx` 0 ms. The three material first-use stalls occurred **inside** `renderer.render`:

| Desktop event | RAF interval | Render | New links | `gpuFinish` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| First exterior shot, `introCut` shot 0 | 6,784.9 ms | 6,750 ms | 4 (depth, terrain and other physical world variants) | 29 ms |
| Exterior → cabin, shot 1 | 3,317.4 ms | 3,291 ms | 4 (two physical, two textured basic world variants) | no multi-second finish |

The eight light slots stayed present; changing their sources was not correlated with program-count growth. Audio, `introShotCam`, `updateIntro` and `endIntro` were at most milliseconds in those frames. `gl.finish()` was generally tens of milliseconds (with a separate 101 ms follow-up), so the multi-second blocks occurred in the renderer's program/driver path, **not** deferred beyond the measured call.

### Targeted intro load fix

Only M1's landing intro now renders its three already-authored views—exterior, cabin and the first gameplay pose—once **behind a temporary `PREPARING DEPLOYMENT` cover** before advancing cinematic time. One initial cheap frame with `scene.visible = false` lets Firefox paint that cover before any heavy world render; the scene is restored on the next prewarm step or an early debug skip. One prewarm step occurs per *rendered* frame even when LittleJS runs multiple fixed updates. Static geometry and the eight local light slots are reused; no mission-transition or movement-time rebuild was introduced. A one-time `gl.finish()` between those covered views ensures their program work is settled before uncovering the intro. The original shot cameras, briefing timing, crew visibility and landed-ship state are restored for the visible cinematic. The cover is also removed when the intro is skipped through a debug hook.

The fix makes the unavoidable first-use work a visible initial load rather than interrupting the active spaceship shots. It does not claim to reduce total initial shader-compilation cost or guarantee a shorter New Campaign start on desktop hardware. A diagnostic-only short cabin far plane merely moved compilation to the following exterior shot and was removed.

In two post-change Firefox 155 software-WebGL runs, covered prewarm steps were roughly 5.7–6.4 s, 1.5–2.2 s and 6.8–8.3 s. **Visible `introCut` shots 0–4 all linked zero shaders and took 16.7–33.3 ms**; the first gameplay frame linked only small residual depth/effect programs (~100–233 ms) rather than the earlier multi-second handoff. `tests/transition-diagnose.mjs firefox` asserts all five visible cuts remain link-free and M2→M3/M4 result stay link-free. `tests/intro-smoke.mjs firefox` and its Edge equivalent passed the real uninstrumented New Campaign intro: the cover cleared, shots advanced and the ship remained `LANDED_LOCKED`. These are **different browser versions/environments** from the supplied Firefox 156 desktop trace, so the on-device gate still needs a desktop retest.

The regression harness `tests/facility-regression.mjs firefox` passed M1→M4 continuity, Mission Select, Continue after reload, M4 purge/boarding/result, Endless, collision, sampled routes and static facility identity. Its fast A–E traversal reported **zero world builds and zero collision-grid rebuilds**, all eight light slots retained. Dynamic encounter allocations can occur during the test; they are not a facility reconstruction. The 272-frame route and software WebGL are not directly comparable to the 327-frame desktop/baseline route or desktop Firefox frame cadence.

On the final standalone software-WebGL runs, both Firefox and Edge linked **zero programs during the 272-frame A–E traversal** (Firefox p95 83.3 ms, worst 500.1 ms; Edge p95 33.5 ms, worst 166.7 ms). There were no facility rebuilds or solid-grid rebuilds. The 500 ms headless Firefox interval did not link shaders; it does not establish desktop frame cadence. Before the targeted prewarm, one previously unseen material linked at the facility corner and cost ~2.7 s in software Firefox. Parallel Firefox/Edge benchmark runs are not valid timing comparisons due to CPU contention.
